Checkpoint64 2.0 is the biggest update since launch. Every screen has been
redrawn, and there's a new way to play together: **Checkpoint Connect** lets
your team join a game server running on your PC through Steam. Nobody forwards
a port. We've also added a handful of smaller things people have been asking
for since the summer.

Here's what's in it.

## A new look: the shelf

The old app was a header bar across the top and a grid of cards that each tried
to carry every button. It worked, but it looked like a settings page, and it
never looked like a place where you keep things.

2.0 is built around a **shelf**.

- **Each game gets a plank, and each save is a floppy standing on it.** The
  floppy shows the save's name, a one-line status and an AUTO light. AUTO shows
  its state by shape and in words, not only by colour.
- **Click a floppy and its details open in an inspector** beside the shelf: back
  up, restore, launch, edit files, the auto-backup switch and the full version
  timeline. On a narrower window it slides in as a drawer instead.
- **The top header is gone.** A sidebar on the left holds your libraries
  (personal and every team), the main pages and a storage meter for the library
  you're in. To switch libraries, click one. You no longer need a dropdown.
- **The logbook docks to the bottom of the window** and opens upward. Every
  entry is now a plain sentence, and clicking one opens that save in the
  inspector.

Every other screen was redrawn to match:

- **Add a game** puts what's installed on this PC next to the full catalogue,
  side by side. Before you create a save, it tells you what the first backup
  will do: roughly how much it will upload, and how much of your storage it
  will use.
- **Settings** is one page with a menu down the side (General, Connections,
  Usage, Billing, My feedback, and Your data & account). Your storage and plan
  stay in view on every settings page.
- **Teams** shows one card per team, with its members, its storage and its
  latest event. You can accept invitations and redeem join codes on that page
  without leaving it.
- **First run** used to be a nine-step tour that ended with "now go add a
  game". Now it opens on the games already installed on your PC and takes you
  straight into adding one. It also answers the two questions people actually
  have before they pick anything: has anything been uploaded yet, and what
  happens to your files.

If you already finished the tour, you won't see the new first run. You can
replay it any time from Settings.

## Checkpoint Connect: play on a teammate's server, no port forwarding

This is the one we're most excited about.

Many co-op groups end up with one friend who runs the server. For everyone else
to join, that friend has to forward a port on their router. That goes badly if
you're on a flatmate's router, a student network or a mobile connection, or
behind CGNAT, where the ISP shares one public address across many customers and
there's no port to forward.

**Checkpoint Connect carries the connection over Steam instead.** The host's
Checkpoint64 accepts connections from teammates through Steam, and passes them
to the game server on their own PC. Each teammate's Checkpoint64 opens the same
port on their own machine, and their game connects to it as if the server were
local. Steam connects the two directly when it can, and through Valve's relays
when it can't. Nobody forwards anything, and CGNAT stops mattering.

How it works in the app:

1. **Start your game's server on your PC**, the same way you do today, either
   the game's own "host" option or a dedicated server exe.
2. **In your team's library, hit _Share a server_.** Pick the game. For many
   games we already know the ports, so they're filled in for you. For anything
   else, pick _Other game_ and type the ports.
3. **Your teammates see "{you} is hosting {game}" with a JOIN button** in the
   same library. The team also gets a green **LIVE** tag in everyone's sidebar,
   so they know a server is up even from another page.
4. **They hit JOIN, and point their game at the `127.0.0.1` address it shows**,
   with a copy button.

A few things worth knowing:

- **It's your server; we don't host anything.** Checkpoint64 shares a server
  that's already running. It never starts one, and there's no server of ours in
  the middle. For more on doing without a dedicated server entirely, see the
  [dedicated server alternative](/dedicated-server-alternative/) guide.
- **Only your team gets in.** When someone connects, the host's app checks with
  us that their Steam account belongs to a member of that team. If it doesn't,
  they're turned away.
- **Your read-only members can join too.** That includes Patreon supporters and
  community members who came in with a join code. Hosting needs a full member.
- **UDP and TCP both work.** That covers games like
  [Valheim](/games/valheim/guide/) and [Factorio](/games/factorio/guide/) over
  UDP, and [Terraria](/games/terraria/guide/) or OpenTTD over TCP. We've played
  real sessions through it with a friend on a different network.
- **It works on every plan.** Valve's relays don't cost us per byte, so there's
  no reason to put it behind a paywall.
- **It's in the Steam version of the app only**, because it runs on Steam's
  networking. It's marked **BETA**, and we can pause it remotely if something
  goes wrong. If that happens, the app says hosting is paused. It won't say you
  were removed.

The host panel shows each connected teammate with their ping and data rates. If
a teammate's connection drops, the app reconnects them automatically, and their
game session carries on.

Checkpoint Connect sits on top of what the app already did for your team:
it doesn't replace it. When the host logs off for the week, the world is still
[on the shelf for the next person to pick up](/co-op/).

## And more

**Pin a version.** Found the save from right before the boss, or the world as
it was before a redesign? Pin it. Automatic cleanup will never delete a pinned
version, and a manual delete asks you to unpin it first. You can pin from the
inspector without opening the full history.

**Controller support.** You can now use the whole app with a gamepad. The D-pad
or left stick moves between controls, A presses, B backs out, the bumpers step
through everything in order, and the right stick scrolls. On a Steam Deck, A on
a text field brings up Steam's on-screen keyboard. The pad only acts while
Checkpoint64 is the focused window, so it won't react while you're playing.

**New saves keep a sensible amount of history.** New saves used to start on
"keep everything", which filled up storage faster than anyone expected. They
now start on "keep the last few backups", set below your plan's limit so you
have room to raise it. Existing saves are unchanged. Anything you've already
backed up stays exactly as it was.

**Clearer when you're out of space.** If your library is full, a banner on the
shelf says so, rather than an error on one backup. Auto-backup also stops retrying a backup
that the quota has already refused.

**System notifications toggle.** If you'd rather Checkpoint64 didn't send
desktop notifications, you can now turn them off in Settings. They're on by
default.

**37 more games.** Eighteen more co-op games, plus nineteen new presets that
include server ports for Checkpoint Connect. Subnautica and S.T.A.L.K.E.R.:
Call of Pripyat saves are now found correctly for Steam players. See
[every supported game](/games/).

**Fixes.**
- The Linux `.deb` installs on Debian 12 again.
- Steam users no longer get logged out and stuck on a login screen they can't
  use.
- A save now shows whose it is, on the floppy and in the inspector.
- Read-only members are never offered a restore they can't perform.

## Same deal as always
