Checkpoint64 v2 is out; host a server for your crew without touching your router

Checkpoint64 started as a way to pass one co-op world around without anybody saving over anybody. Take the lock, play your session, push the world back. That covers most weeks for most groups, and it still does.

But some nights you want the whole crew in the same world at the same time. Until now that meant one of two things: rent a server that sits idle all week, or have whoever hosts spend an evening in their router's admin page, lose, and discover their ISP puts them behind CGNAT anyway.

v2 adds a third option.

Checkpoint Connect

Start the game's server on your own PC, the way you would anyway. Open your team in Checkpoint64, pick the game and hit Share server. Everyone else in the team sees Join in the team's library. They click it, point their game at 127.0.0.1, and they're in.

Between the two PCs, Steam carries the traffic: directly when it can, and through Valve's relays when it can't. Nobody forwards a port and nobody opens a firewall, and CGNAT stops being a problem.

A few things it is careful about:

  • Only your team gets in. When someone connects, the host's app asks our server whether that Steam account belongs to a member of the team, and it lets them in only on a yes. If the answer doesn't come back, the connection is refused.
  • UDP and TCP. That covers Valheim, Palworld, Satisfactory and Project Zomboid, and also the TCP servers, like Minecraft Java and Terraria. Presets carry the ports for two dozen games; for anything else, type them in.
  • It shares a server; it doesn't run one. Checkpoint Connect forwards to a server that is already running on your PC. It never starts one, and we don't host anything. The world is online while your PC is.
  • A dropped teammate gets their seat back. If someone's connection blips, they reconnect on the same address, so the game server still recognises them instead of timing them out.

Checkpoint Connect is in the Steam version of Checkpoint64 on Windows and Linux, Steam Deck included, and it is on every plan, Free too.

It pairs with the lock. When the host logs off, their last backup is the latest world, so the next person can take the lock and host from their own PC. The co-op page walks through it, and every game page with known server ports now says how to skip the port forward.

A redesigned app

Every main screen has been rebuilt: the Library, the save inspector and its file list, the Add a game wizard, Settings, Teams and the first-run screens. The cartridge shelf is still the cartridge shelf, but it reads more clearly. Auto-backup shows its state by shape and in words rather than by colour alone, the lock button only offers Take over to someone who can actually take over, and the logbook writes every event as a plain sentence.

When your storage fills up, the Library now says so in a banner across the top instead of quietly failing the next backup, and the app stops re-sending a backup the quota has already refused.

Play it with a pad

You can now drive the whole app with a controller. The D-pad or left stick moves between things, A picks, B backs out, the bumpers step through the page, and the right stick scrolls. On a Steam Deck it just works, and pressing A on a text field opens Steam's on-screen keyboard. The pad only acts while Checkpoint64 has focus, so it never steals input from the game you're playing next to it.

18 more co-op games

Green Hell, ARK: Survival Ascended, Conan Exiles, Barotrauma, Bellwright, Railroads Online, Hearts of Iron IV, Minecraft Bedrock, Desynced, Ore Factory Squad, Saleblazers, Incursion Red River, Allumeria, Anime Shop Simulator, Dimraeth, Eden Crafters, MISERY and Waterpark Simulator all have presets now. Like every catalog change, they appear on your Add screen without an update.

Smaller things

  • Games whose save folders hold the odd extra file (Minecraft's icon.png, Baldur's Gate 3's modsettings.lsx, a Unity Player.log) back up again. A file-type check was refusing those files and failing the whole backup.
  • Settings has a System notifications switch, on by default, if you'd rather the app never raised an OS notification.

Updating

If you have Checkpoint64 already, it updates itself: the Steam version through Steam, the direct download in the app. Your saves, teams and history carry over untouched.

Download Checkpoint64 or grab it on Steam. It is free to start, and Checkpoint Connect is in the Steam version on every plan.
